Discriminator Validity & Generalization — v4

2026-07-27 · Companion to RESULTS_BD_V4.pdf. Question under test: does the unseen (e99) discriminator genuinely generalize to a new scenario, or does the downstream seen≈unseen equality hide something? All discs here are the v4 round (_v4), trained on the EOS-fixed backbones' pools; the pre-fix (v2) numbers appear only in the confound case study (§4). The body reports classification accuracy; AUC for the same runs is in Appendix A.

1. What the discriminator is, and split hygiene

  • Role: guidance needs the block-conditional ratio w = qexcept(x₀|x<b)/pθ(x₀|x<b); the disc D estimates it as D/(1−D) on clean partial paths [prefix ∥ block], adjacency-conditioned (GraphSAGE embedding of Ascn + per-position edge-existence features), bounded logit ℓ = 4·tanh(z/4).
  • Seen (e0): positives = except_0 paths, 1% of the pool after excluding the 1,000 eval-reserved rows = 19,308 paths (RandomState(777) permutation). Unseen (e99): positives = except 1–99 only (1% per scenario × 99 = 1,911,492 paths); except_0 never contributes a gradient. The except_0 validation accuracy printed during training is a monitoring diagnostic only (fixed 4,000 steps, no model selection).
  • Every evaluation below uses held-out data for both discs: except_0 probes use the reserved rows; except_1 probes use rows outside e99's 1% slice; negatives are fresh (seed-99 pools disjoint from the seed-1 training pools; fresh normal sample for the data-negative probe).

2. Symmetric probe design and metrics

Two probe scenarios flip the roles: on except_0 the e0 disc is in-scenario and e99 is zero-shot; on except_1 e99 is in-scenario (held-out rows) and e0 is zero-shot. If generalization is real, the in-scenario advantage should be small and should flip with the probe.

  • Accuracy = hit rate on a balanced set of 1,000 positives + 1,000 negatives, thresholding at logit > 0 (i.e. D > 0.5, the natural BCE threshold — never tuned). Chance = 0.500.
  • Zero-shot drop = (accuracy on the probe that is not the disc's training scenario) − (accuracy on its own scenario): exc1 − exc0 for e0, exc0 − exc1 for e99. More negative = more damage from moving scenario.
  • Logit Pearson = correlation of the two discs' logits over the identical 2,000 inputs — direct evidence of learning the same function.

4. The negative-pool confound — v2 blk1 case study

Under the pre-fix (v2) backbones, the blk1 disc posted the best accuracy (0.943) while blk2 posted the worst (0.766) — an apparent non-monotonicity. The cause was the negative pool, not the disc: v2 blk1's unconditional generations were degenerate (every path ran to the canvas cap, length 101 vs real 23.2; 50.5% invalid edges), so its negatives were separable by a length shortcut alone. After the EOS fix the blk1 pool is healthy (length 25.2; 2.3% invalid) and blk1 accuracy falls to an honest 0.760 — the ordering is now monotone in the pools' invalid-edge rates at every block. Lesson: with model-negative training, per-block scores conflate disc quality with generation quality; only the within-block seen-vs-unseen comparison (same pool for both discs) is confound-free.

5. Data-negative discs (block-independent): the strict generalization probe

Positives = real except paths, negatives = real normal paths — real-vs-real, so every scenario-agnostic shortcut (model artifacts, length) is removed and only scenario-specific information (which edges were removed, how routes divert) separates the classes. One disc per regime, no per-block pools, hence no §4 confound. These discs involve no backbone at all, so they are identical across v2/v4 (same seed).

  • This probe separates the training regimes: the single-scenario e0 disc drops −0.085 / −0.099 zero-shot, while the 99-scenario e99 disc is flat (−0.023 / +0.026). Genuine scenario-specific generalization is a property of multi-scenario training — one scenario does not teach the adjacency-conditioning rule well enough.
  • Absolute levels are lower because the task is genuinely hard (fam 0.05, e0 zero-shot 0.641 against a chance of 0.500): telling which scenario produced a real shortest path is a subtle question.
  • The logit correlation also drops on the zero-shot side (exc1: 0.737 / 0.834) — where the task is scenario-specific, the two discs really are different functions.

6. Why downstream seen ≈ unseen even so

  • Guidance uses the model-negative disc, whose signal transfers almost freely (§3) — the hard probe of §5 concerns a disc variant the pipeline does not deploy (the §4.1b ablation in the v3 report).
  • Self-normalized IS cancels calibration: weights wn = exp(γℓn) enter only as wn/Σwm, so any constant logit offset multiplies out exactly. What remains is ranking damage, and that is small for the deployed disc (e99 zero-shot accuracy drop ≤0.020, AUC drop ≤0.015 — Appendix A).
  • At guidance time the scored inputs are model candidates, i.e. exactly the distribution the model-negative disc was trained against; the deployed disc is matched to its use.

7. Conclusion

The seen/unseen equivalence observed downstream (v∧a gap ≤0.015 at fam 0.05, ≤0.003 at fam 0.1 across all blocks) is fully accounted for: (1) the deployed model-negative discriminators transfer because their discrimination signal is scenario-agnostic by construction and adjacency is an input (e99 zero-shot accuracy drop ≤0.020); (2) SNIS guidance is invariant to the calibration shift zero-shot transfer causes; and (3) independently, the strict real-vs-real probe shows the e99 recipe acquires transferable scenario-specific knowledge that single-scenario training does not (−0.023 vs −0.085). The one caveat for reading disc metrics — per-block scores reflect negative-pool difficulty — is demonstrated and resolved by the v2→v4 blk1 case study.
