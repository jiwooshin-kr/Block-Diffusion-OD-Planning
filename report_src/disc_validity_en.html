Discriminator Validity & Generalization — v4

2026-07-27 · Companion to RESULTS_BD_V4.pdf. Question under test: does the unseen (e99) discriminator genuinely generalize to a new scenario, or does the downstream seen≈unseen equality hide something? All discs here are the v4 round (_v4), trained on the EOS-fixed backbones' pools; the pre-fix (v2) numbers appear only in the confound case study (§4).

1. What the discriminator is, and split hygiene

  • Role: guidance needs the block-conditional ratio w = qexcept(x₀|x<b)/pθ(x₀|x<b); the disc D estimates it as D/(1−D) on clean partial paths [prefix ∥ block], adjacency-conditioned (GraphSAGE embedding of Ascn + per-position edge-existence features), bounded logit ℓ = 4·tanh(z/4).
  • Seen (e0): positives = except_0 paths, 1% of the pool after excluding the 1,000 eval-reserved rows (RandomState(777) permutation, first 1,000 reserved). Unseen (e99): positives = except 1–99 only (per-scenario 1%), except_0 never contributes a gradient; the except_0 validation accuracy printed during training is a monitoring diagnostic only (fixed 4,000 steps, no model selection).
  • Every evaluation below uses held-out data for both discs: except_0 probes use the reserved rows; except_1 probes use rows outside e99's 1% slice; negatives are fresh (seed-99 pools disjoint from the seed-1 training pools; fresh normal sample for the data-negative probe).

2. Symmetric probe design

Two probe scenarios flip the roles: on except_0 the e0 disc is in-scenario and e99 is zero-shot; on except_1 e99 is in-scenario (held-out rows) and e0 is zero-shot. If generalization is real, the in-scenario advantage should be small and should flip with the probe — which is exactly what happens. Metrics: AUC, accuracy@0, and Pearson correlation between the two discs' logits on identical inputs.

  • Transfer is cheap here by construction: on this task most of the signal — edge legality against the input adjacency, plus model-generation artifacts — is scenario-agnostic. The disc reads Ascn as an input, so "an edge absent from the given adjacency ⇒ fake" transfers to any scenario.

4. The negative-pool confound — v2 blk1 case study

Under the pre-fix (v2) backbones, the blk1 disc posted the best AUC (0.985) while blk2 posted the worst (0.881) — an apparent non-monotonicity. The cause was the negative pool, not the disc: v2 blk1's unconditional generations were degenerate (every path ran to the canvas cap, length 101 vs real 23.2; 50.5% invalid edges), so its negatives were separable by a length shortcut alone. After the EOS fix the blk1 pool is healthy (length 25.2; 2.3% invalid) and the blk1 AUC falls to an honest 0.870 — now the AUC sequence is monotone in the pools' invalid-edge rates at every block. Lesson: with model-negative training, per-block AUCs conflate disc quality with generation quality; only the within-block seen-vs-unseen comparison (same pool for both discs) is confound-free.

5. Data-negative discs (block-independent): the strict generalization probe

Positives = real except paths, negatives = real normal paths — real-vs-real, so every scenario-agnostic shortcut (model artifacts, length) is removed and only scenario-specific information (which edges were removed, how routes divert) separates the classes. One disc per regime, no per-block pools, hence no §4 confound. These discs involve no backbone at all, so they are identical across v2/v4 (same seed).

  • This probe separates the training regimes: the single-scenario e0 disc degrades substantially zero-shot (−0.100 / −0.084), while the 99-scenario e99 disc is nearly flat (−0.022 / +0.018). Genuine scenario-specific generalization is a property of multi-scenario training — one scenario does not teach the adjacency-conditioning rule well enough.
  • The logit correlation also drops on the zero-shot side (exc1: 0.737 / 0.834) — where the task is scenario-specific, the two discs really are different functions.

6. Why downstream seen ≈ unseen even so

  • Guidance uses the model-negative disc, whose signal transfers almost freely (§3) — the hard probe of §5 concerns a disc variant the pipeline does not deploy (it is the §4.1b ablation in the v3 report).
  • Self-normalized IS cancels calibration: weights wn = exp(γℓn) enter only as wn/Σwm, so any constant logit offset — the main casualty of zero-shot transfer (accuracy@0 drops much more than AUC) — multiplies out exactly. Only the ranking/relative differences matter, and those transfer (AUC gap ≤0.05).
  • At guidance time the scored inputs are model candidates, i.e. exactly the distribution the model-negative disc was trained against; the deployed disc is matched to its use.

7. Conclusion

The seen/unseen equivalence observed downstream (v∧a gap ≤0.015 at fam 0.05, ≤0.003 at fam 0.1 across all blocks) is fully accounted for: (1) the deployed model-negative discriminators transfer because their discrimination signal is scenario-agnostic by construction and adjacency is an input; (2) SNIS guidance is invariant to the calibration shift that zero-shot transfer does cause; and (3) independently, the strict real-vs-real probe shows the e99 training recipe does acquire transferable scenario-specific knowledge that single-scenario training does not. The one caveat for reading disc metrics — per-block AUCs reflect negative-pool difficulty — is demonstrated and resolved by the v2→v4 blk1 case study.
